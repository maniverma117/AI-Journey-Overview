# 7. KV Calculation

Suppose, purely for illustration:

```text
80 layers
8 KV heads
128 head dimension
BF16
```

KV per token:

```text
80 × 8 × 128 × 2 × 2 bytes
```

Approximately:

```text
327,680 bytes/token
```

≈

```text
320 KB/token
```

At:

```text
40K tokens
```

that's roughly:

```text
320 KB × 40,000
≈ 12.8 GB
```

for one active sequence.

This is why long-context workloads can become KV-capacity problems.

---

> "H200 is better."

> "The additional memory may materially improve long-context concurrency and potentially reduce tensor parallelism. I'd benchmark H100 and H200 under the 40K-token P99 workload before selecting the fleet."

# PART X — Does P/D Make Sense?

Customer asks:

> "Should we use prefill/decode disaggregation?"

Don't immediately say yes.

Ask:

```text
Is prefill causing TTFT problems?

Is decode causing TPOT problems?

Are their resource profiles materially different?

Can we independently scale them?

Is KV transfer fast enough?

Do we have RDMA/EFA?

Does the complexity produce measurable SLO benefit?
```

If:

```text
short context
low RPS
balanced workload
```

then:

```text
coupled vLLM
```

is probably better.

If:

```text
very long context
large prefill workload
strict TTFT
decode SLO
strong P/D imbalance
```

then:

```text
P/D
```

becomes more interesting.

Current vLLM documentation explicitly describes disaggregated prefilling as experimental and notes that it can independently tune TTFT and ITL but does not inherently improve throughput.

# PART XLI — The CTO Challenge

The CTO says:

> **"I see 24 H100s. Why not 4?"**

Your answer:

> "Four GPUs aren't enough to provide the required SLO-constrained capacity and failure tolerance for the workload you've described. The 70B benchmark shows TP4 sustains 25 RPS while meeting the latency SLO. Your 120-RPS peak requires 150 RPS of planned capacity with our 25% headroom assumption. Six TP4 replicas provide 150 RPS, and retaining five replicas after one replica failure still provides 125 RPS, preserving the peak requirement. That's why the current baseline is 24 GPUs. We'd validate the final number against the production 40K-token workload distribution and actual HBM/KV behavior before procurement."

That's a **customer-defensible answer**.

# PART XLII — The CFO Challenge

> **"Why can't we reduce GPU utilization target from 60% to 90%?"**

Answer:

> "We can investigate whether additional utilization is economically beneficial, but I wouldn't optimize utilization independently of the latency and availability SLO. Running closer to saturation can increase queueing and tail latency and leave less capacity for failures or bursts. The target should be minimum total cost while maintaining the required SLO and failure capacity."

# PART XLIII — The Security Architect Challenge

> **"Can the LLM execute AWS actions?"**

> "The LLM can propose tool calls, but it cannot directly authorize them. Every action passes through a Tool Gateway with tenant identity, authorization, resource restrictions, parameter validation, risk policy and audit. AWS access uses least-privilege workload identity, and high-risk operations can require human approval."

---

# PART XLIV — The Platform Engineer Challenge

> **"Why not just round-robin the vLLM pods?"**

Answer:

> "Because inference endpoints aren't stateless from a performance perspective. Queue depth, KV utilization and prefix locality affect request cost and latency. For a sufficiently distributed serving fleet, inference-aware routing can make better decisions than ordinary round-robin."

---

# PART XLV — The ML Engineer Challenge

> **"Why not increase max_num_seqs to maximize GPU utilization?"**

Answer:

> "Increasing concurrency can improve batching and throughput until memory, scheduler or latency constraints become dominant. Beyond the sustainable operating point, queueing, KV pressure and tail latency can increase. I'd benchmark the saturation curve rather than optimize max_num_seqs in isolation."

---

# PART XLVI — The DevOps Challenge

> **"Why not just use HPA on CPU?"**

Answer:

> "CPU isn't the primary resource governing LLM inference. The important signals include token demand, queueing, TTFT, TPOT, KV capacity and GPU state. HPA can still be the scaling mechanism, but the metric should represent inference demand rather than generic CPU load."

---

# PART XLVII — The Networking Challenge

> **"Why do you need EFA?"**

Answer:

> "Only if the architecture requires high-performance GPU communication such as multi-node tensor parallelism or efficient P/D KV transfer. For a single-node inference architecture without those requirements, EFA may add unnecessary complexity."

Excellent.
